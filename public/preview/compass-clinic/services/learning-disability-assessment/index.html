Learning disability assessment

Completed through a psychoeducational assessment of cognitive skills and academic skills in reading, math and writing.

For
Children and youth
Format
In clinic; also on outreach trips
To start
Call to book. Bring report cards and any earlier reports.

A learning disability, also called a learning disorder, is diagnosed when a child shows core difficulties in one or more of three foundational areas: reading, mathematics or written expression.

What has to be true for a diagnosis

  • The academic difficulties persist despite targeted intervention and support.
  • Academic skills are substantially below what is expected for the child's age and cause problems at school, at work or in everyday activities.
  • The difficulties are not better explained by another concern, such as an intellectual disability, vision or hearing problems, a medical condition, lack of instruction, or difficulty speaking or understanding the language.

Learning disabilities are described by severity: mild, moderate or severe.

How we assess

The assessment is completed through a psychoeducational assessment. It evaluates cognitive skills, including overall intelligence, and academic skills across reading, mathematics and written expression. Children with a learning disability generally have average or higher cognitive ability; their difficulty is not a lack of potential. Interviews, clinical observation and a review of report-card history are part of the picture.
