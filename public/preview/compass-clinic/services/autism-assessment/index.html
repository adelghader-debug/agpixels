Autism assessment

Diagnostic assessment for children, youth and adults, following BCAAN standards, using the ADOS-2 and ADI-R.

For
Children, youth and adults
Format
In clinic; a multidisciplinary team for children under 6; also on outreach trips
To start
Call to book an intake. Under 6, the assessment includes pediatric, psychological and speech components.

Autism spectrum disorder refers to a wide range of conditions characterized by differences in social communication, repetitive behaviours and restricted interests, and sensory sensitivities. It usually appears in the first three years of life and can often be reliably identified before the age of two, but many adults go undiagnosed for years, especially when their traits were subtle, masked or misunderstood.

How the assessment works

All autism assessments for diagnosis at Compass Clinic follow the BC Autism Assessment Network (BCAAN) standards and are eligible for access to Provincial Autism Funding. Early intervention matters, and a private assessment can help families access funding earlier than the public waitlist allows.

  • Intake and consultation. We review developmental history and current concerns and determine the right assessment pathway based on age and BCAAN standards.
  • Under age 6: multidisciplinary assessment. A pediatrician, a developmental or IQ assessment completed by a psychologist, a speech-language assessment, and a clinical diagnostic assessment (CDA) that includes the ADOS-2 and the ADI-R.
  • Age 6 and over, and adults: clinical diagnostic assessment. Conducted with standardized, evidence-based tools by clinicians trained in the ADOS-2 and ADI-R.

Adult autism assessment

Adults often seek assessment after a lifetime of feeling different, struggling in relationships, or finding that other therapies have not fully addressed their needs. Our adult assessments are neurodiversity-affirming and evidence-based, and are meant to help you understand your strengths and challenges and find the right support and accommodations.

Early signs parents often notice

A child does not need to show all of these, and none of them is a diagnosis on its own:

  • Not responding to their name by 12 months of age
  • Not pointing to objects to share an interest by 14 months
  • Avoiding eye contact and preferring to be alone
  • Not speaking, or using very few words, at 18 months
