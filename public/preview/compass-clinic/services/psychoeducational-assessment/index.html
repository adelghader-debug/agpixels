Psychoeducational assessment

A detailed picture of how a child learns: intelligence, academic skills, attention, executive functioning, memory and behaviour.

For
Children and youth (ages 2 to 18 for psychometry)
Format
In clinic, usually one full morning or afternoon of testing; also on outreach trips
To start
Call to book. Testing is booked as a 4-hour block; the full process takes 10 to 14 hours of the psychologist's time.

A psychoeducational assessment identifies a child's learning style and needs. Parents often request one after witnessing or hearing about difficulties at school. The assessment helps identify strengths, weaknesses and alternative learning approaches that may give a child a better educational experience. It is also the route to diagnosing ADHD, learning disabilities and giftedness.

What may be tested

  • Intelligence
  • Academic functioning: reading, math, written expression and phonological awareness
  • Attention and concentration
  • Executive functioning and planning
  • Behavioural strengths and challenges
  • Memory

Input from parents and teachers is gathered through standardized questionnaires.

The process

  1. Clinical interview. A comprehensive interview with you to discuss concerns, history, medical issues and living situation.
  2. Testing. Your child completes a series of standardized tests with observation throughout, and is interviewed about their own concerns and emotional well-being. We prefer to do the testing over one day with recess breaks, because that gives the most realistic picture of a school day. You book 4 hours; it may run longer or shorter depending on how fast the child works. The psychologist may suggest splitting the day if the child cannot manage the duration.
  3. Scoring and analysis. Behind the scenes, the psychologist scores the tests, reviews the file and report cards, and identifies patterns.
  4. Feedback meeting. Parents (and the child, if old enough) walk through the report with the psychologist, including how the diagnostic conclusions were reached.

The total time ranges from 10 to 14 hours depending on the complexity of the presentation.

Payment and what to bring

The assessment is usually paid in two installments, one after the initial assessment and one after the feedback session; other installment plans can be arranged. Full payment is required before the final report is released. The office accepts cash, credit, debit and cheque.

  • A detailed list of any medications and supplements your child takes
  • Report cards or any academic history describing the areas of concern
  • Your child's medical records from any hospital, psychiatrist, therapist or doctor

Note: our assessments are comprehensive, but we do not offer neuropsychological assessments, which are specialized assessments completed by a neuropsychologist where a brain injury is known or suspected.
