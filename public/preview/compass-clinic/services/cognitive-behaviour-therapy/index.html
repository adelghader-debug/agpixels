Cognitive behaviour therapy

A short-term, structured approach that works on the connection between thoughts, emotions and behaviours.

For
All ages
Format
In clinic; generally 5 to 20 sessions
To start
Call to book counselling and ask for CBT.

Cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT) is a widely used and well-researched approach. It can be an effective treatment for anger, anxiety (generalized and social), phobias and panic, depression, eating concerns, obsessive compulsive disorder, relationship problems, sleep difficulties and trauma.

How it works

CBT promotes change by examining the connection between thoughts, emotions and behaviours. It is often not events themselves but the meaning we give them that shapes how we see ourselves; part of CBT is challenging negative thoughts and correcting misinterpretations. It also focuses on skillful problem-solving so that clients respond to feelings in healthy ways.

What to expect

CBT is short-term and solution-focused, grounded in a client's present reality, and structured around goals set in the first sessions. Homework is part of therapy, so skills get practised in daily life. Therapy generally ranges from 5 to 20 sessions.
