Occupational therapy

Assessment and family-centred therapy for the everyday activities a child finds hard: self-care, school work, play.

For
Children and families
Format
In clinic, one-on-one or in groups
To start
Call to book. Ask about coverage; some plans cover OT.

Occupational therapy is sought when everyday activities that matter to a child or family meet with frustration. These fall into self-care (dressing, bathing, feeding), productivity (printing, school work, community involvement) and leisure (biking, playground play, keeping up with peers).

How we help

An occupational therapist assesses what is preventing the child from participating in the activity. That may identify motor challenges, sensory differences, or environmental factors at home, school or daycare. Using a family-centred approach, the OT works with the child and family on the limitations identified and coaches parents so they can support their child's continued growth.

Our occupational therapists collaborate with other members of the team, including speech-language pathologists, and can provide additional assessments and treatment specific to your needs. Your OT has a university education, often a master's degree, and is registered with the College of Occupational Therapists of BC.

Concerns we work with

  • Developmental coordination disorder
  • Developmental disabilities, including autism spectrum disorder, ADHD and Down syndrome
  • Mental health concerns
  • Sensory processing differences and self-regulation challenges
  • Executive dysfunction
